# Ubuntu — The Five Pillars and Five Principles

*Umuntu ngumuntu ngabantu* — a person is a person through other persons.
In Shona: *Ndiri nekuti tiri* — I am because we are.

Ubuntu is the philosophical substrate of the Mukoko ecosystem. It is not a node inside the 3D architecture; it is the ethical ground beneath every node. The Nyuchi Design System encodes Ubuntu in three five-fold expressions: the Five **Questions** (a decision filter), the Five **Pillars** (the spheres a person lives within), and the Five **Principles** (the virtues a person carries). Two hands of five fingers. One grip.

## The Five Pillars — *where* Ubuntu is situated

The Pillars name the spheres of relationship a person is held within. Ubuntu does not begin with the individual; it begins with belonging. A person becomes a person through these five concentric spheres, each broader than the last.

| # | Shona | English | Sphere |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Mhuri** | Family | Immediate relationships — kin, household |
| 2 | **Nharaunda** | Community | Extended relationships — neighbours, peers, chosen kin |
| 3 | **Vanhu** | Society | Broader human web — strangers, institutions |
| 4 | **Zvakatipoteredza** | Environment | The natural world that holds all relationships |
| 5 | **Unhu** | Spirituality | Meaning, purpose, the vertical axis of being human |

The Pillars are derived from Mugumbate et al. (2023), who identify family, community, society, environment, and spirituality as the broad levels of Ubuntu. The full live records — including each pillar's platform surface in Mukoko — are stored in the `genetic-code-ubuntu-pillars` collection and surfaced via the MCP tool `get_ubuntu_pillars`.

## The Five Principles — *how* Ubuntu is embodied

The Principles name the behavioural virtues a person practises. Where the Pillars describe context, the Principles describe conduct. Mbigi (1997) articulated them as the Collective Fingers Theory, anchored in the Shona proverb *chara chimwe hachitswanyi inda* — "one finger cannot crush a louse."

| # | Shona | English | What it asks of us |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Kurarama** | Survival | Collective endurance. No one survives alone. |
| 2 | **Kubatana** | Solidarity | Active commitment to the common good. |
| 3 | **Tsitsi** | Compassion | Active care extended beyond kin to all. |
| 4 | **Ruremekedzo** | Respect | Unprejudiced consideration of every person. |
| 5 | **Chiremerera** | Dignity | Inherent worth held by virtue of existence. |

Full records — including each principle's expression in Mukoko features — are stored in the `genetic-code-ubuntu-principles` collection and surfaced via `get_ubuntu_principles`.

## The Five Questions — the decision filter

Every product decision in Mukoko passes through five questions, stored in `styling-meta.philosophy.ubuntuQuestions`. The Questions are the runtime filter; the Pillars and Principles are the structural foundation the Questions rest on. Together they form the three five-fold expressions of Ubuntu in the doctrine.

## Why restorative justice is not here

Restorative justice is not one of the five principles. It is a modern American framework developed in juvenile justice and therapeutic discourse, not an African one. Ubuntu addresses harm through the continuous practice of the virtues above, not through a separate remedial process. The community does not define itself by how it handles wrongdoing — it defines itself by how it lives.

## Scholarly provenance

The Pillars and Principles are derived from published African scholarship. They are not invented for the platform.

- Mugumbate, J., Mupedziswa, R., Twikirize, J., Mthethwa, E., Desta, A., & Oyinlola, O. (2023). *Understanding Ubuntu and its contribution to social work education in Africa and other regions of the world.* Social Work Education, 43(4), 1123–1139.
- Mbigi, L. (1997). *Ubuntu: The African Dream in Management.* Pretoria: Sigma Press.
- Samkange, S., & Samkange, T. M. (1980). *Hunhuism or Ubuntuism: A Zimbabwe Indigenous Political Philosophy.* Salisbury: Graham Publishing.
- Ramose, M. (2002). *The Philosophy of Ubuntu as a Philosophy.* Oxford University Press.
- Du Toit, A., Poovan, N., & Engelbretch, A. (2006). Additional attribution for compassion.
- Poovan, N., et al. (2006). Additional attribution for respect and dignity.

Mbigi was Zimbabwean. He died in Harare in June 2023. The derivation is sovereign.
