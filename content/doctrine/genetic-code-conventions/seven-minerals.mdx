# The Seven Minerals — colour as contract

> The Nyuchi colour system is seven minerals dug from African earth. Each mineral carries a role, and the role is a contract: choosing a colour is choosing what that colour is responsible for. The seven split into two families — what the ground gives and what the hand has always worked — and the families are a claim about how the future sits on the past.

This convention records the seven-minerals doctrine: the canonical palette, the role each mineral carries, and the two-family grouping. It supersedes the five-mineral palette referenced in older conventions. The colour *values* live as token rows in `styling-minerals`; this document records the *meaning* — the rules a value alone cannot carry.

## The seven minerals

| Mineral | Role | Family | Origin |
| -- | -- | -- | -- |
| Cobalt | Knowledge | deep-earth | Katanga Copperbelt — DRC & Zambia |
| Sodalite | Intelligence | deep-earth | Kunene River — Namibia & South Africa |
| Tanzanite | Identity | deep-earth | Merelani Hills — Tanzania |
| Malachite | Growth | deep-earth | Congo Copper Belt |
| Gold | Value | hand | Ghana · South Africa · Mali · Zimbabwe |
| Copper | Stewardship | hand | Central African Copperbelt — Zambia & DRC |
| Terracotta | Community | hand | Pan-African Sahel |

Two minerals were added to the original five: **Sodalite** (the intelligence blue, Shamwari's colour) and **Copper** (the stewardship orange, Bundu's colour). The colours are not chosen from a palette; they are pointed to on a geological map of the continent.

## Thesis one — the role is the contract

Every mineral carries a one-word **role**. The role is not decoration or mood; it is a functional contract. To use a colour is to invoke its role.

| Mineral | Role | What invoking it commits to |
| -- | -- | -- |
| Cobalt | Knowledge | links, calls-to-action, anything that teaches or opens a door |
| Sodalite | Intelligence | AI and Shamwari surfaces, reasoning, deep-thinking states |
| Tanzanite | Identity | the Mukoko signature — super-app shell, social surfaces, who-you-are |
| Malachite | Growth | success states, health, positive change, the green light to go |
| Gold | Value | commerce, payments, achievements, the moment value changes hands |
| Copper | Stewardship | Bundu, the commons, the foundation everything stands on |
| Terracotta | Community | Ubuntu, belonging, anywhere people gather |

The rule: **a designer choosing a colour is also choosing a role.** Picking gold means marking a moment of value exchange — not adding a yellow accent. Picking sodalite means surfacing intelligence — not decorating with blue. If the role does not fit the surface, the colour does not belong on it. This is the colour-system equivalent of tri-mode's three-question test: the colour must answer "what am I responsible for here?" or it is the wrong colour.

### Brand → mineral

Each ecosystem brand inherits one mineral as its primary, and the inheritance follows the role, not the aesthetic:

- **Mukoko** → Tanzanite (Identity) — the super-app is where people are themselves
- **Nyuchi** → Gold (Value) — infrastructure that is worth paying for
- **Shamwari** → Sodalite (Intelligence) — the sovereign AI
- **Bundu** → Copper (Stewardship) — the ecosystem that holds the rest

The full brand→mineral mapping for all ecosystem brands lives in `styling-ecosystem`.

## Thesis two — two families, one earth

The seven minerals split into two families, and the split is a claim, not a colour-wheel convenience.

**From below — the deep earth** (`deep-earth`): Cobalt, Sodalite, Tanzanite, Malachite. The minerals of the modern economy, pulled from the ground — the metals and stones that power batteries, circuits, and the digital age. Cool, luminous colours. They carry the surfaces of the future the continent is building: digital, analytical, social, growth.

**From the hand — what we have always worked** (`hand`): Gold, Copper, Terracotta. The warm metals and fired clay that human hands have shaped for millennia. Gold worked into ornament, copper drawn into connection, clay fired into vessel and wall. Warm, grounded colours. They carry value, stewardship, and belonging — commerce, the commons, the gathering.

The claim: **the future the continent builds (deep earth) sits on a foundation of what the continent has always worked (the hand).** One family is not above the other. The deep-earth minerals would be inert without the hand that works them; the hand has nothing to shape without what the earth gives. The two families are tuned to read together in the same composition — a deep-earth cobalt CTA on a hand-family terracotta ground is not a clash, it is the thesis rendered in pixels.

## The heritage tones (the parallel seven)

Beneath the seven minerals sits a parallel set of seven **heritage tones** — Indigo, Savanna, Baobab, Sunset, River, Hematite, Kalahari — the colours of the landscape *above* the ground rather than the minerals *below* it. They live in `styling-heritage-colors` and provide atmosphere, neutral anchors, and mini-app surfaces. Minerals carry role and family; heritage tones do not — they are environment, not contract. Below and above, fourteen colours describe a whole continent, rhyming with the 7/14 spacing and radius scales.

## The rule (the test)

For any surface, component, or token reference that uses a mineral colour:

1. **What role am I invoking?** Name the one word. If you cannot, you are using the colour decoratively, which is a smell.
2. **Does the role fit the surface?** A payment button is Value (gold). An AI reasoning indicator is Intelligence (sodalite). A community space is Community (terracotta). Mismatches are rejected.
3. **Which family does this surface belong to, and is the composition honouring the two-families relationship?** Deep-earth and hand should read together, not fight.

Heritage tones are exempt from the role test (they are environment), but not from the palette — raw hex outside the canonical token files is rejected regardless.

## Why it matters

A brand palette is usually a marketing artefact — a set of swatches chosen to feel a certain way, reassigned whenever fashion shifts. Treating colour as contract makes it an architectural artefact instead: the cobalt that highlights a primary button in a consumer app is the same cobalt, with the same Knowledge role, that highlights an active row in an enterprise dashboard. Same colour, same role, same contract — across every surface in the ecosystem, governed at the Foundation level, queryable from the DB. That is what lets a colour mean something rather than merely look like something.
